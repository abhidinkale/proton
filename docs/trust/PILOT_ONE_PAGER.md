## The problem

Your physicians spend the last hour of every clinic day writing notes about the day they just had. Documentation is the single largest source of avoidable admin time in ambulatory care, and it is the main reason clinicians leave.

## What this pilot is

Eight weeks. Three to five of your physicians use Proton, an AI copilot that drafts the note while the consultation happens. The physician edits and signs. **No note is saved without a physician's approval.**

**It runs on synthetic patients.** Proton ships with three invented patient records — not your patients, not de-identified versions of your patients. Your clinicians use those to test whether the drafting, the safety checks, and the review workflow fit how they actually work.

This is a **usability and workflow evaluation**, not a clinical trial and not a production deployment.

## How we will judge it

Targets, not promises. If we miss them, the readout says so.

| Metric | Target |
| :--- | :--- |
| Median note-drafting time vs. baseline | 50% reduction |
| Drafts accepted with minor edits only (no full rewrite) | 70% or more |
| Clinician-rated usefulness | 4 out of 5 or better |
| Safety gate catches reviewed and judged correct | 90% or more |
| Sessions completed without a support call | 80% or more |
| Real patient data entered | **Zero. Any occurrence ends the pilot.** |

## Exactly what data is involved

**Synthetic:** the three invented patient records, and the consultation transcripts your clinicians speak during the exercise.

**Real:** the participating clinicians' names, work email addresses, and professional role; timestamps and usage events; their ratings and written feedback.

**Not involved:** any record of any real patient. Not de-identified records, not "just one" record, not a screenshot of a record. The pilot environment contains no real patient data and is not connected to your EHR.

If a real record is entered, we stop, delete it, and tell you the same day.
